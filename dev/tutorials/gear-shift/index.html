    Tutorial: Gear-shift logic¶
    You will learn an automatic gearbox's shift schedule, hysteresis, a State Chart in its own ECU task, kickdown and hills, fuel consumption per gear
    Before this Your first model: a cruise control, A car on a drive cycle; read State machines first
    Level and time intermediate, about 60 minutes
    Follow along start the editor (uv run nervus), File ▸ Open examples/gear_shift.nervus, press ▶ and double-click the scopes; the figures below come from the same model
    Topic Powertrain and engine control ▸ Transmission and driveline (all tutorials)

    This tutorial follows examples/gear_shift.py: the shift logic of an automatic gearbox, running in its own ECU (the TCU, transmission control unit), and a car to try it on. You will see how a shift schedule works, why it needs hysteresis, and how it reacts to the driver and to a hill.

    uv run python examples/gear_shift.py      # simulates, saves gear_shift.nervus, runs the tests
uv run nervus examples/gear_shift.nervus  # open it in the editor

    The model¶

    The pedal and the road grade come from From Workspace blocks. The tcu subsystem reads the vehicle speed and the pedal and decides the gear; the car subsystem turns the gear and the pedal into speed and engine speed.

    The shift schedule¶

    A TCU decides the gear from two maps over (gear, pedal position):

    Upshift speed [km/h] pedal 0 % 50 % 100 %
    1 → 2 15 25 40
    2 → 3 30 45 70
    3 → 4 45 65 100
    4 → 5 60 85 130
    Downshift speed [km/h] pedal 0 % 50 % 100 %
    2 → 1 10 15 30
    3 → 2 22 32 55
    4 → 3 35 50 85
    5 → 4 50 68 110

    With more pedal both thresholds rise. The engine revs further before each upshift, so there is more power when the driver asks for it. With a light foot the gearbox shifts up early, and the engine runs at low speed, where it uses less fuel. Pressing the pedal fully at speed raises the downshift speed above the current speed, and the gearbox drops a gear or two: kickdown.

    In the TCU, two 2-D Lookup Tables (up_map, down_map) read the maps for the current gear and the pedal. The current gear comes from the chart through a Unit Delay: the maps for this step use the gear of the last step, which also breaks the loop between the chart and the maps. The maps are workspace variables (upshift_map, downshift_map), so they are calibration data in the generated C code and in the A2L file.

    The chart¶

    input speed, up_speed, down_speed
output gear = 1, shifting = 0
param min_gear_time, shift_time

state g1
  entry: gear = 1; shifting = 0
state g2
  entry: gear = 2; shifting = 1
  during: shifting = 1 if t_in_state < shift_time else 0
...
transition g1 -> g2: speed > up_speed and after(min_gear_time)
transition g2 -> g3: speed > up_speed and after(min_gear_time)
transition g2 -> g1: speed < down_speed and after(min_gear_time)
...

    One state per gear. Two things keep the gearbox from hunting (shifting up and down again and again):

    1. Hysteresis in the maps. Every downshift speed is below the upshift speed of the gear below. After an upshift from 2nd to 3rd at 45 km/h (50 % pedal), the speed would have to drop to 32 km/h before the gearbox goes back to 2nd.
    2. A minimum time in each gear, after(min_gear_time): 100 samples of 10 ms = 1 s.

    While a shift is in progress (shift_time = 0.3 s) the output shifting is 1 and the engine torque is cut. A real gearbox does this to synchronise the speeds of the shafts.

    The TCU is an atomic subsystem with a 10 ms sample time, so it is one ECU task. Generate its C code with Code → Generate C on the selected tcu block, or:

    from nervus.codegen import generate_c

code = generate_c(d.block("tcu"))

    The car¶

    gearbox: the ratio of the current gear times the final drive, \(i = i_{gear}\,i_{final}\). The engine speed follows from the wheel speed, \(n = \frac{v}{r_{wheel}}\,i \cdot \frac{60}{2\pi}\) rpm. The clutch is locked, except below idle speed, where it slips (clutch_slip takes the maximum of the two).

    engine: the torque is the pedal fraction times the full-load torque at the current speed, minus the friction torque. With the pedal released, the friction brakes the car (engine braking). While shifting, the torque is cut to zero.

    body: Newton's second law along the road, with aerodynamic drag, rolling resistance and the grade \(\alpha = \arctan(\text{grade}/100)\):

    \[ m\,\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{T\,i\,\eta}{r_{wheel}} - \tfrac{1}{2}\rho\,c_d A\,v^2 - m g\,c_{rr}\cos\alpha - m g \sin\alpha \]

    Fuel consumption: why light-pedal upshifts save fuel¶

    The engine has a brake-specific fuel consumption map (BSFC, in g/kWh) over speed and torque. The fuel flow is the map value times the power the engine delivers:

    \[ \dot m_{fuel} = \frac{\mathrm{BSFC}(n, T)\cdot T\,\omega}{3.6\cdot 10^{6}} \quad [\text{g/s}] \]

    The map is best (about 245 g/kWh, 33 % efficiency) at middle speed and high load, and poor at light load, where the throttle losses dominate. Cruising at 80 km/h needs the same power in every gear, but in 5th the engine runs at 2000 rpm with a high load, in 3rd at 3700 rpm with a light load. Over the cruise (12-22 s):

    engine speed fuel
    5th gear (the shift schedule) 2040 rpm 5.1 L/100 km
    held in 3rd (upshift_map row 3 = 999) 3720 rpm 7.6 L/100 km

    Half as much fuel again, for the same speed. The model integrates the fuel (fuel_g) and the distance (distance); the whole drive with its full-throttle launch, kickdown and hill uses about 14 L/100 km.

    Results¶

    • Full-throttle launch (0–9 s): the upshifts come at 40 and 70 km/h, at almost 5000 rpm. Each upshift drops the engine speed, and the speed curve has a short flat step: the torque cut.
    • 20 % pedal (9–22 s): the thresholds fall below the current speed, so the gearbox shifts straight up to 5th and the engine settles at about 2100 rpm.
    • Kickdown (22 s): at 100 % pedal the downshift speeds of 5th (110 km/h) and 4th (85 km/h) are above the 76 km/h of the car. The gearbox drops to 4th, and one second later (the minimum gear time) to 3rd. The engine jumps to 3700 rpm and the car accelerates.
    • A 10 % hill at 60 % pedal (from 28 s): the car slows down and, below 76 km/h, the gearbox drops to 4th.

    The four test cases in the Test Manager check each phase. The test in tests/test_examples.py also checks that no gear is held for less than a second, and that the C code of the TCU gives exactly the same gears as the simulation.

    Experiments¶

    To try one: change the value in the Model Workspace tab (double-click it), or for a block parameter select the block and edit it in Properties; press ▶ and compare with the previous run in the Data Inspector (Ctrl+I). Ctrl+Z undoes the change.

    Try What you will see
    min_gear_time = 0 the kickdown goes from 5th to 3rd within 0.07 s, and the launch shifts 4 → 5 after only 0.2 s in 4th
    shift_time = 1.0 longer torque cuts: the car reaches 71 instead of 80 km/h at 9 s
    upshift_map 1 → 2 at full pedal = 60 (first row: 15, 25, 60) 1st gear tops out at 55 km/h, where the engine reaches 6400 rpm and its full-load torque runs out: the car is stuck in 1st until the pedal is released at 9 s. Shift maps must fit the gear ratios
    mass = 2500 the heavier car never reaches 5th: the kickdown drops one gear, and the hill pulls it down to 3rd
    gear_ratios = [4.0, 2.4, 1.6, 1.2, 1.0] shorter gears: 85 instead of 80 km/h at 9 s, but 2800 instead of 2100 rpm when cruising
