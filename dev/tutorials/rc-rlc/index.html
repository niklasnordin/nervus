    Tutorial: RC and RLC circuits¶
    You will learn time constant and step response, cut-off frequency and the Bode plot, resonance, damping and the quality factor, physical circuit blocks and linear analysis
    Before this Your first model: a cruise control
    Level and time beginner, about 30 minutes
    Follow along start the editor (uv run nervus), File ▸ Open examples/rc_filter.nervus, examples/rlc_circuit.nervus, press ▶ and double-click the scopes; the figures below come from the same model
    Topic Electrics and electronics ▸ Circuit fundamentals (all tutorials)

    The first lab of every electronics course: charge a capacitor through a resistor, then add an inductor and watch the circuit ring. examples/rc_rlc.py builds both circuits from physical blocks, and you measure their step response in the time domain and their frequency response with Linear Analysis, and compare with the formulas.

    uv run python examples/rc_rlc.py      # builds rc_filter.nervus and rlc_circuit.nervus

    The RC low-pass¶

    Open examples/rc_filter.nervus: an In block (5 V) drives a controlled voltage source, through \(R = 1\) kΩ into \(C = 1\) µF; a voltage sensor measures the capacitor and feeds the Out block. The capacitor starts empty, so pressing ▶ shows the classic charging curve:

    \[ v_C(t) = V \left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau = RC = 1 \text{ ms} \]

    After one time constant the capacitor is at 63 % (3.16 V), after five at 99 %. The model's test case checks both.

    Now Analysis ▸ Linear Analysis (Ctrl+L), from v_in to v_out: the transfer function is \(G(s) = 1 / (\tau s + 1)\), a first-order low-pass. The Bode plot is flat at low frequencies and falls by 20 dB per decade above the cut-off frequency

    \[ f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi RC} = 159 \text{ Hz}, \]

    where the gain is −3.01 dB (half the power) and the phase −45°.

    The RLC circuit¶

    Open examples/rlc_circuit.nervus: the same source, 1 V, through \(R = 10\) Ω, \(L = 10\) mH and \(C = 10\) µF in series. The capacitor voltage is now a second-order system:

    \[ G(s) = \frac{1}{LC s^2 + RC s + 1}, \qquad f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}} = 503 \text{ Hz}, \qquad \zeta = \frac{R}{2}\sqrt{\frac{C}{L}} = 0.16 \]

    With so little damping the step response overshoots by 60 % and rings at about 500 Hz before settling, and the Bode plot has a resonance peak of +10 dB at \(f_0\): the quality factor \(Q = 1/(2\zeta) = 3.2\), the voltage across the capacitor at resonance is 3.2 times the input.

    The resistance decides the damping. Critical damping (\(\zeta = 1\)) needs \(R = 2\sqrt{L/C} = 63\) Ω: the fastest response without overshoot. More resistance makes it overdamped: no overshoot, but slower.

    Experiments¶

    Change the values in the Model Workspace, run, and compare in the Data Inspector or Linear Analysis:

    Try What you will see
    RC: C = 10e-6 τ = 10 ms: ten times slower, and the cut-off moves to 15.9 Hz
    RLC: R = 63.2 critical damping: no overshoot, settled in about 2 ms
    RLC: R = 200 overdamped: slow, like a first-order circuit
    RLC: R = 1 ζ = 0.016: Q = 32, a 30 dB resonance peak and a ringing that lasts many milliseconds
    RLC: L = 2.5e-3 f₀ doubles to 1007 Hz, ζ doubles to 0.32

    From here, the circuits tutorial goes on to diodes, transistors and switching converters.
